I went to Tra Vinh today. it was a really long day today, I started waiting for almost half an hour under the scorching heat of the city for my ride and went on it for over 5hrs. When I arrived, I had to travel around to visit distant relatives on my father's side. 
One thing I noticed while traveling to Tra Vinh the first time since I think 5 or 6 years, is that all the paddy fields are mixed crop now, they aren't purely rice but rather mixed with maize or sugarcane. I guess it's to hedge when one crop doesn't yield well or do well on the market. 

Another thing I can feel is that my father's hometown feels like one of those [[Life at the Frontlines of Demographic Collapse]]. That is to say everyone is old and everyone who is taking of the elderly is also old. They work hard, physically, laboriously. By the standard of city life. But they didn't get the social safety net of a developed country like Japan. Some of them are borderline disable, that is, it's hard for them to even climb down the stair in front of their house. So I dread 10 to 20 years from now, what will be made of these pre-unification towns. Since the town I am at in Tra Vinh was founded during thr French colonial period.
Another thing I noticed is that most of the road has been paved now, as opposed to dirt road like I remember the last time I came. Also, most people has rebuilt their home to big and impressive style, although most people living in them are old, they seem like mass retirement home, but with physical labor.

The air quality is top notch and the sky visibility is crystal clear. I can see all the stars in the sky, as opposed to the light polluted cityscape.

Business is booming, at less the funerary services like funeral service or grave renovation service. I saw multiple grave engraving businesses as well as coffin sellers. Seems like people come here to die. 

I haven't had any sleep since the 4hrs I had yesterday and I haven't showered until 10 p.m now. It's been a busy day.
